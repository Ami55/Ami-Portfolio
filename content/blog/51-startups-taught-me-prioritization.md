# What Startups Taught Me About Prioritization

Startups rarely have a shortage of ideas. They have a shortage of time, stable systems, and people who can own every good idea at once.

That environment taught me to treat implementation capacity as part of SEO strategy.

## The biggest opportunity may be the wrong first project

A large content program may have impressive potential, but not if the product positioning changes every month and nobody can maintain the pages. A smaller template fix or clearer information architecture may create a better foundation.

## Recommendations compete with the rest of the company

SEO does not receive a private engineering team. Its work competes with customer issues, product releases, sales needs, and technical debt. A useful recommendation explains dependencies and offers a realistic first version.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What should a startup prioritize first in SEO?**
Start with clear positioning, crawlable foundations, useful core pages, measurement, and opportunities the existing team can maintain.

**Should startups invest in large content programs?**
Only when the topic, audience, ownership, quality standard, and maintenance plan are clear enough to support them.

**How do you make SEO recommendations more likely to ship?**
Reduce scope, clarify impact and dependencies, name an owner, and define a useful first release.
