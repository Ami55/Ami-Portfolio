# The Internal Linking Mistake I See on Growing Websites

“Add more internal links” is common SEO advice. It is not wrong, but it is often too late in the diagnosis.

On a growing website, the deeper problem may be that nobody can explain which page owns the topic.

## Links cannot resolve unclear ownership by themselves

Teams publish similar articles at different times. Product and editorial sections use different language. Regional pages repeat the same introduction. Several pages begin targeting the same intent.

Adding links between all of them creates connection, but not necessarily clarity.

Before recommending links, I map the pages, their purpose, primary entity, target intent, and relationship to a hub. Sometimes the best action is a link. Sometimes it is consolidation, repositioning, or removing a page from the competition.

## Architecture comes before automation

Automated link suggestions are useful only when the system understands which relationships matter. Similar words do not always indicate the right destination.

My [Entity SEO Internal Linking Platform](https://seogirl-entity-seo-internal-linking-platfor.vercel.app/) was built around this practical need: connect pages through entities and roles, while keeping editorial judgment in the process.

## What I would check on Monday

- Assign one primary purpose to each important page.
- Identify pages competing for the same intent.
- Define hubs, supporting pages, and next step pages.
- Review orphan pages and pages with excessive links.
- Write anchors that explain the relationship honestly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do more internal links always improve SEO?**
No. Useful links can support discovery, context, and navigation, but excessive or poorly chosen links can make the architecture less clear.

**How do I choose the correct destination page?**
Choose the page that best owns the entity, intent, or next step represented by the anchor and surrounding passage.

**Should similar articles always be merged?**
No. Merge them when they compete for the same job without a meaningful distinction. Separate pages can remain useful when their intent and role are clear.
