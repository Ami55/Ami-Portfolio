# First-Hand Experience Is Not a Personal Paragraph Added at the End

I have seen articles where a short personal paragraph is added after everything else is written.

“In our experience, this is important.”

The sentence sounds more human, but it does not tell the reader what anyone actually experienced. Nothing in the recommendation changes. No new detail appears. The article remains a summary with a personal label attached.

## Real experience changes the content

If someone has genuinely done the work, they usually know details that affect the decision.

They know where the process becomes slower than expected. Which input is often missing. Which error message is misleading. Which step sounds easy in documentation but creates trouble in practice. Which customer question appears after publication.

Those details should influence the structure of the article, not sit in a decorative box at the end.

## Ask about moments, not opinions

“What do you think about this topic?” often produces a broad answer.

I get better material from narrower prompts:

- Tell me about the last time you did this.
- What went wrong first?
- What did you expect to happen?
- What did you measure or record?
- What would you prepare differently next time?
- Which advice would have saved you time?

The answers contain scenes, decisions, and useful exceptions.

## Not every experience belongs in the article

A personal detail should earn its place by helping the reader understand or decide something. The goal is not to make a technical guide about the author.

## What I would check on Monday

- Identify who has actually performed the process.
- Interview them before finalizing the outline.
- Ask for a recent example, not only general advice.
- Capture constraints, evidence, and unexpected outcomes.
- Use only details that improve the reader’s decision.
- Label the limits of the experience honestly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does first-hand content need to use “I” throughout the article?**
No. Experience can appear through documented steps, observations, photographs, measurements, examples, and specific advice without making every paragraph personal.

**Can AI generate first-hand experience?**
No. AI can help organize material supplied by people, but it should not invent events, tests, quotes, or observations.

**Is a customer story first-hand information?**
It is first-hand for the customer. The company should attribute it clearly and avoid presenting the customer’s experience as its own.

**How much experience should one article include?**
Include enough to answer the reader’s practical questions. Quality and relevance matter more than the number of anecdotes.
