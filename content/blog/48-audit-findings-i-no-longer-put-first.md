# The Audit Findings I No Longer Put at the Top

Crawlers are very good at finding countable problems. Missing metadata, long titles, redirect chains, duplicate headings, and broken links deserve attention. But the largest count does not always deserve the first slide.

## I no longer lead with what is easiest to export

If forty thousand pages are missing from sitemaps, a list of two hundred long title tags is not the opening story. If one template is producing poor LCP across a large section, a handful of isolated warnings should not distract from it.

I begin with patterns that affect discovery, page ownership, important templates, customer trust, and the business’s ability to act.

## Severity needs context

A technically severe issue on an unused page may matter less than a moderate issue repeated across the primary customer journey. I consider scale, page value, evidence, risk, effort, and ownership together.

## The first slide should support a decision

Leadership does not need a crawler interface copied into a deck. It needs to know what is happening, why it matters, what should happen next, and what tradeoff is involved.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Should small technical issues be ignored?**
No. They should be documented and scheduled appropriately without displacing higher impact patterns.

**How do you rank audit findings?**
I compare likely impact, affected scale, confidence, risk, effort, dependency, and ownership.

**Why group findings by template?**
Template patterns reveal repeatable causes and often create more efficient implementation paths than URL level fixes.
