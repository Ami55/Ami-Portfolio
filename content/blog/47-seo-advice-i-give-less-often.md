# The SEO Advice I Give Less Often Now

Some SEO advice is popular because it is short enough to remember. That does not make it wrong, but it can make it dangerously easy to apply without context.

## “Publish more content”

I say this less often because many sites already have content they cannot classify, maintain, or connect. I first ask whether the missing opportunity needs a new page or a better job for an existing one.

## “Make the article longer”

Length is sometimes a side effect of answering the question properly. It is not the purpose. A page can become longer while the useful answer becomes harder to find.

## “Add more internal links”

I now ask which page owns the topic and what relationship the link should communicate. More links cannot repair a confused architecture by themselves.

## “Implement all the schema”

I prefer the schema that truthfully expresses visible, maintained information. Extra markup does not compensate for contradictory content.

## “Everything is high priority”

This is the advice I try hardest to avoid. A recommendation becomes useful when the team can compare its likely impact, confidence, effort, risk, and ownership.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are these recommendations always bad?**
No. Each can be correct in the right situation. The problem is using a familiar action before diagnosing the real constraint.

**How should SEO advice be prioritized?**
Connect it to evidence, business impact, implementation effort, confidence, risk, and a clear owner.

**What replaces universal advice?**
A testable explanation of the problem and a recommendation suited to the site’s actual context.
