# What Travel SEO Taught Me About Entities, Locations, and Useful Depth

Travel websites contain an enormous number of relationships. A tour belongs to a destination, visits attractions, may suit a particular traveller, operates in a season, and is led by a guide with local experience.

If those relationships are unclear, adding more destination adjectives will not fix the page.

## Place content needs a real job

I have worked on descriptions for tours, attractions, destinations, and guide led experiences. The strongest content does not repeat that a place is beautiful or unforgettable. It helps someone understand what the place is, why it matters, how it connects to the experience, and what decision comes next.

## Scale makes entity consistency important

On a global marketplace, the same attraction may appear across tours, city pages, guides, and articles. Preferred names, location relationships, and meaningful internal links help those pages work as a system.

This is also why the 40K page sitemap gap mattered. If valuable travel entities exist but discovery systems do not represent them, good content remains hidden inside the platform.

## Local experience creates information gain

Travel content becomes useful when it includes details a generic summary cannot provide: realistic timing, neighbourhood context, seasonal tradeoffs, accessibility, local customs, and practical choices from people who know the place.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is an entity in travel SEO?**
It can be a destination, attraction, tour, guide, organization, event, neighbourhood, or other identifiable thing with attributes and relationships.

**How much detail should a travel page include?**
Enough to support its specific user decision. Useful depth is not the same as covering every possible travel question on one page.

**Why are internal links important for travel sites?**
They help travellers and search systems understand relationships among destinations, attractions, tours, guides, and supporting information.
